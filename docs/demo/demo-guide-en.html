Aitra Meter: a per-model electricity meter for AI inference

One 8-GPU server runs eight of the newest Qwen3.5/3.6 models while Aitra Meter measures, live, how many joules each model spends per generated token — and a console lets you reshape the traffic on stage with the boards responding in seconds. This guide covers the environment, the measurement principles, the workloads, the console, the dashboards, and conference Q&A.

How the metering works

Analogy: an apartment building has one master electricity meter and eight tenants. Aitra Meter installs a sub-meter per tenant, reads it every window, and issues per-tenant bills — the "tenant" is a model, and the unit price is J/token: joules per generated token. Core rule: energy is counted per GPU, GPUs are grouped by the pod that holds them, and summing happens only inside a group.

The headline metric: J/token

Joules per token — how much energy the GPU spends for every token a model generates. Lower is better. For intuition:

  • The smallest model at baseline: 1.8 J/token — a million tokens ≈ 0.5 kWh;
  • The largest dense model at baseline: 13.6 J/token — a million tokens ≈ 3.8 kWh;
  • Fully batched, measured as low as 0.07 J/token — a million tokens ≈ 0.02 kWh.

The reading cycle (every 5-second window)

  1. Who holds which GPUs — read the kubelet device-plugin checkpoint (Kubernetes records this anyway; zero configuration — new models enrol automatically, departed ones disappear);
  2. What each GPU spent — per-GPU cumulative hardware energy counters from dcgm-exporter, differenced against the previous window;
  3. What each model produced — each pod's own vLLM /metrics token counter, likewise differenced;
  4. The bill — per model: energy of exactly its GPUs ÷ its tokens (TP pods sum exactly their k GPUs). GPUs held by nobody are booked separately as idle — never split among tenants.

Display semantics (what the boards will and won't show)

SituationJ/token showsWhy
Normal serving window (≥ 30 tokens)the measured valueboth numerator and denominator are statistically meaningful
Window under 30 tokens (traffic boundary, deep queue)0a handful of tokens against a hot GPU reads absurdly high — noise, never displayed
Model holds a GPU but has zero traffic (idle demo)0 (power stays truthful)boards must reflect now, not freeze at a stale value; the resting wattage stays visible on the power panels
GPUs held by no podbooked as the idle residualtrue standby cost, charged to nobody

One-line doctrine: a non-zero J/token is a trustworthy measurement; anything untrustworthy reads zero. There is no in-between. A rolling CV (coefficient-of-variation) gate < 3 % backs every quoted number.

Verify it yourself, live

Since the window length cancels out, J/token ≈ watts ÷ tokens-per-second. Measured example (0.8B under load): 199.3 W ÷ 2294 tok/s = 0.087 J/token — matching the board's 0.09. Anyone can audit any tile this way at any moment.

Engineering details: components and degradation
  • Checkpoint parsing accepts both NUMA-map and flat layouts; intersected with Running pods to drop terminated leftovers;
  • Energy counters clamp on reset; a failed scrape skips the tick and the next one spans the gap — no energy lost;
  • Model naming: vLLM model_name label → pod labels (aitra-ai.github.io/model-name / model / app) → pod name;
  • Unreachable pod endpoints (model loading, non-vLLM) count zero tokens but still pay for their GPUs;
  • Processes sharing one GPU (MPS/time-slicing) cannot be split by hardware counters — attribution degrades to token-proportional and is labelled attribution_method: proportional; prefill/decode phase energy is an explicit non-goal;
  • Agent RBAC is read-only pods/nodes; runAsUser: 0 only because the checkpoint directory is root-owned.

What's running: models, traffic, two states

The single most important insight: J/token is a property of model × load, not of the model alone. Every model below gets two numbers — the baseline (concurrency ≈ 1, a conservative upper bound) and the loaded value (sustained batching, the production lower bound). The gap is your optimisation headroom.

3.2 Four ready-made storylines

  • The size ladder — 0.8B → 27B: ~7.5× more energy per token as parameters grow;
  • Quantisation A/B — the same Qwen3.6-27B in bf16 vs FP8: 38 % saved per token, identical hardware and traffic;
  • Sparse activation — both MoE models punch far below their weight class: the 35B-A3B costs about what a 2B dense costs. You pay for activated parameters, not total parameters;
  • Batching dividends shrink with size — small models drop 20×, the 27B only 8×: small models have more idle silicon to amortise. A direct scheduling insight.

The demo console: your cockpit

The whole show runs with zero command line: open meter.aitra.ai/console?k=<key> (bilingual zh/EN), make four picks, press one button — the boards respond within seconds.

4.2 Scenes and global switches

  • 📈 Sweep 1→32 (~12 min) — steps concurrency for the selected model(s): the Demo C descent curve on demand;
  • 💓 Burst ×8 — 30 s full load / 60 s quiet: heartbeat curves, the crowd-pleaser;
  • ⏻ Baseline toggle — off = fleet-wide idle (J/token drops to zero, power shows true resting draw); on = back to normal. One round trip is a complete idle-cost demo;
  • ■ Stop all — the kill switch; back to baseline within a window.

4.3 A 15-minute script you can follow verbatim

  1. Steady-state opening — Demo A: eight tiles in their lanes; tell the size-ladder and MoE stories;
  2. Idle — baseline off: within a minute everything reads zero while power reveals the resting floor ("idle silicon still burns money");
  3. Recovery + single-model dive — baseline on; pick 0.8B, Real conversations × c64 × continuous: watch 1.8 → 0.09 ("same GPU, 20× cheaper per token");
  4. Comparison experiment — multi-select 27B + 27B-FP8, same load: quantisation savings side by side, live;
  5. Heartbeat finale — select all eight, Burst; then Stop all, and the room goes calm.

Security boundary: access-key gated (7-day cookie); RBAC pinned to namespace Jobs + scaling exactly one Deployment (the loadgen); every job hard-capped and TTL-cleaned; one job per model, eight globally; all dimensions validated server-side.

6.5 Red flags (stop the segment)

Two tiles tracking identically → attribution leak; switch to the recorded loop, debug offline.

TP=1 reading machine-level energy → idle GPUs leaking into the numerator.

CV persistently > 3 % → don't quote that model's number; check for intermittent traffic first.

Tokens at zero while power persists → the service lost traffic, not the meter; check the baseline toggle and job list.

FAQ — conference Q&A prep

Ordered by how likely the question is at an open-source conference; every answer is short enough to deliver from memory.

Project positioning

QA
What is Aitra Meter and what problem does it solve?An open-source energy meter for AI inference (a Prometheus exporter): in clusters where many models share GPUs, it attributes every joule to the specific model that spent it and reports J/token plus derived cost and carbon. It fixes the blind spot of "one big GPU power bill, nobody knows which model ran it up."
How is this different from Kepler / DCGM-exporter / Scaphandre?Those report power at the hardware or container level. Aitra Meter adds the layer that matters for inference: per-model attribution joined with token output — numerator (measured energy of exactly that model's GPUs) and denominator (that model's own tokens) are both isolated per model, yielding a genuinely comparable efficiency unit. We consume DCGM as a data source, and Kepler integration is on the roadmap (issue #48).
Why J/token as the unit?It's the "fuel economy" of inference: comparable across models, hardware and load, and multiplies straight into cost and carbon. We're also proposing gen_ai.infrastructure.energy.* to the OTel GenAI semantic conventions (issue #38) to make it a shared industry vocabulary.
License and status?Apache-2.0, github.com/aitra-ai/aitra-meter, v0.8 line, one-command Helm install. Issues and PRs welcome — the booth runs mainline code.

How it works

QA
How do you attribute energy to a single model?Three steps: ① read the kubelet device-plugin checkpoint for "which pod holds which GPUs" (Kubernetes records this anyway — zero config); ② difference each GPU's hardware cumulative energy counter (DCGM) per window; ③ "GPUs group by holding pod; summing only inside a group" — single-GPU models are exact measurements, TP pods sum exactly their k GPUs, idle GPUs are booked separately and never smeared across tenants.
Do I need to change my model or serving code?Zero intrusion. The agent discovers GPU-holding pods automatically and scrapes the engine's existing /metrics. Models appear on the board when they start and vanish when they scale down — at the booth, a third-party platform's model was discovered and metered with no configuration at all.
Measurement or estimation? How accurate?Whole-GPU-per-model is measurement (hardware counters, no modelling): the same model isolated vs co-resident with 7 others differs by ~1 % J/token. Every figure carries a CV stability gate (< 3 %), and windows under 30 tokens display nothing — non-zero means trustworthy.
Is J/token a constant per model?No — it's a property of model × load. The same model can differ 20× between concurrency 1 and 64 (batching amortises the fixed power draw). We always quote numbers with their load context: baseline as the planning upper bound, loaded as the production lower bound.
What's the measurement overhead?Near zero: pure scraping of counters DCGM and the engine already expose — no eBPF, no instrumentation, no sampling profiler. The agent is one small Go process per node at millicore CPU.
Which engines and hardware?Engines: vLLM native; SGLang/Triton/Ollama adapters in review (PR #69/#88); a generic-prometheus adapter for anything exposing a token counter. Hardware: NVIDIA first (DCGM/NVML), an AMD provider exists; per-MIG-slice metering is PR #71.
Tensor parallelism? Shared GPUs?A TP=k pod holds k GPUs → exactly those k are summed; at TP=1 the other GPUs' idle draw does not leak into the numerator (Demo B exists to prove this live). Multiple processes on one GPU can't be split by hardware counters — we degrade to token-proportional sharing, honestly labelled, rather than pretending precision.
Can you split prefill vs decode energy?No, and it's an explicit non-goal: hardware counters have no notion of phase, so any per-phase energy claim is model-based estimation, not measurement. What we show is that workload shape (read-heavy vs write-heavy) moves aggregate J/token — strictly worded.
GPU only? What about CPU/host?Today J/token is GPU-only (the dominant share in inference) and documented as such. Host-side energy (RAPL, chassis) is in PR #84 and will add a full-stack figure.

Adoption and ecosystem

QA
What do I need to deploy it? How fast?Any Kubernetes cluster with the NVIDIA device plugin + dcgm-exporter; one Helm command installs the agent (DaemonSet), aggregator and Grafana boards. This 8-model booth went from zero to metering in under a day; a single-cluster trial shows its first J/token within half an hour.
How does it become money and carbon? Billing?Transparent conversion via site parameters (PUE, tariff, grid intensity) — every figure prints its formula on the dashboard. Namespace-level 30-day chargeback with CSV export feeds internal billing directly.
Does it fit my existing observability stack?Natively: everything is standard Prometheus metrics (the aitra_* family) — Grafana/Alertmanager/Thanos just work; an OTLP exporter ships windows to an OpenTelemetry Collector too.
Is the booth data real?All live and measured: the 8× A100 server is right here running eight Qwen3.5/3.6 models — scan the QR, open meter.aitra.ai, and you're watching their wattage now. Grab the console and reshape the load yourself; the curves respond within ~10 seconds.
What's on the roadmap?Near-term: SGLang/Triton/Ollama adapters, MIG slice metering, host energy, Kepler integration. Mid-term: OTel GenAI energy semantics, efficiency signals for schedulers (llm-d, KAI). Issues are open for adoption.
